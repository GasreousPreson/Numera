# Numerith
Numera is an innovational board game with Numerical territory battles

In **Numerith**, two players compete for territory using a limited set of numbered pieces.  
Higher numbers can overpower the territory controlled by lower numbers.  
Deciding **when to commit high-value pieces, when to conserve them, and how to maximize territorial control** forms the strategic philosophy of the game.

Below is a brief introduction to the board and rules.

## Board
- Board size: **9 × 9**

## Pieces
- Each player has **two complete sets of numbered pieces from 1 to 9**, for a total of **18 pieces per player (36 pieces in total).**

## Basic Rules
- Players take turns placing one piece on an empty square.
- After being placed, each piece controls its **four orthogonally adjacent squares** (up, down, left, and right).

---

## Placement Rule – Numerical Supremacy

- A player may place a piece inside an opponent's controlled area **only if the number of the placed piece is strictly greater than the number of the opponent's controlling piece.**
- Pieces with equal numbers **cannot** be placed within each other's controlled area.
- There is **no numerical restriction** within your own controlled area. You may freely place pieces inside territory controlled by your own pieces.
- Placing a larger number next to a smaller opponent's piece **does not capture or remove that piece**, nor does it eliminate its control. The conflict is resolved only during final scoring.

---

## End of the Game

- The game ends when both players have placed all of their pieces.

---

## Victory

Each player scores the total number of squares they actually control, including:

- Squares occupied by their own pieces.
- Controlled squares.
- If multiple friendly pieces control the same square, it is counted only once.

**The player controlling more territory wins.**

If both players control the same number of squares, the game is a draw.

---

## Territory Resolution

### 1. Overlapping Control

Example:

3️⃣ □ 4️⃣

The empty square between the two pieces is controlled by both players.

- The player with the **higher number** gains the point for that square.
- The player with the lower number scores nothing.
- If the numbers are equal, **both players score that square.**

---

### 2. Adjacent Pieces

Example:

3️⃣4️⃣                

The two occupied squares are controlled by both players.

- Both occupied squares are awarded to the player with the **higher-numbered piece**.
- The player who owns **smaller-numbered piece** will lost this two spatial scores
